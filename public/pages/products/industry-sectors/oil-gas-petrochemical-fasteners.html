Material Engineering

The grade pairing matrix buyers actually need.

Stud and nut grades must be specified together. Service temperature, H₂S exposure, chloride environment, heat treatment and project hardness limits decide the final combination.

A193 B7 A194 2H Cr-Mo alloy steel, quenched & tempered.
Workhorse refinery / process bolting. High-pressure / elevated-temperature flange and vessel service.
A193 B7M A194 2HM / 7M* Controlled-hardness alloy steel.
Used where sour-service requirements call for lower hardness. Sour / H₂S applications only within validated ISO 15156 / project envelope.
A193 B16 A194 project pairing Higher-temperature alloy steel. Elevated-temperature process / steam duties where selected by design.
A320 L7 A194 4 / 7* Low-temperature alloy steel with impact requirements. Low-temperature process, LNG support systems, cold service.
A320 L7M A194 7M* Low-temperature + controlled-hardness variant. Sour + low-temperature combinations where project conditions permit.
A193 B8 A194 8 304 austenitic stainless family. Corrosion-resistant service; class/condition determines strength.
A193 B8M A194 8M 316 austenitic stainless family. Chloride-bearing / corrosive environments, project-dependent.
Duplex 2205 Matching CRA High-strength chloride-resistant duplex stainless. Offshore, seawater, chemical and marine exposure.
Super Duplex 2507 Matching CRA Higher pitting / crevice corrosion resistance. Severe offshore, subsea, seawater and chloride service.
Nickel Alloys Matching alloy Inconel / Monel / Hastelloy families. Highly corrosive or extreme-temperature chemical environments.

Important engineering notes

NACE / sour service: never label a fastener simply “NACE compliant.” Compliance depends on exact material condition, hardness, heat treatment and the actual H₂S service envelope.
Stainless galling: austenitic stainless assemblies can gall. Nut/bolt pairing, lubrication and installation method must be engineered together.
Exotic alloys: PMI, heat/lot segregation and strict material traceability become commercially critical for duplex and nickel-alloy packages.
Service Selection Matrix

Choose bolting by environment, not by habit.

These are common engineering directions, not substitutes for project design. Final grade, coating and bolting stress must follow the governing code, service conditions and operator specification.

Refinery / Petrochemical

High-pressure flanges, valves, exchangers, columns and pressure equipment.

Typical direction: B7 / B16 / B8M + matched A194 nuts; PTFE or project coating where maintainability is important.

Sour Gas / H₂S

Wet H₂S creates sulfide stress cracking risk.

Typical direction: B7M / L7M or suitable solution-treated CRA only when validated against ISO 15156 / ISO 17945 conditions.

Offshore / Marine

Salt spray, chloride exposure, splash zone and marine atmosphere.

Typical direction: 316L / duplex / super duplex / nickel alloy or engineered high-performance coating system.

LNG / Cryogenic

Low-temperature fracture resistance and impact properties become central.

Typical direction: A320 low-temperature grades or suitable stainless / nickel alloy as specified.

Pipeline / Midstream

Valves, stations, compressor skids, supports and pigging facilities.

Typical direction: B7/B7M/L7 families, HDG/PTFE/zinc-flake depending exposure and operator specification.

Severe Chemical Duty

Highly corrosive process fluids and aggressive chemical combinations.

Typical direction: duplex, super duplex, Inconel, Monel or Hastelloy families with matched nuts and PMI.
Coating & Friction Engineering

Corrosion performance and torque behavior are one system.

In critical bolting, coating thickness, pretreatment, lubricant and friction coefficient determine both corrosion life and preload consistency. A coating should never be chosen on color alone.

PTFE / Xylan-Type

Premium refinery / offshore option

Low friction, good chemical resistance and strong disassembly performance where correctly specified.

  • Typical project colors: blue / green / custom
  • Often used over phosphate or metallic base systems
  • Torque values must match actual friction system

Zinc Flake

Thin-film corrosion protection

Excellent choice for high-strength parts where electrolytic hydrogen charging is a concern.

  • Geomet / Dacromet-type families
  • Good film uniformity
  • Can be paired with controlled-friction topcoat

Zinc-Nickel

High-performance electroplate

Strong corrosion performance at low thickness; baking and hydrogen-control requirements remain important.

  • Good for compact thread geometry
  • Passivate / sealer system matters
  • Project approval required for critical service

Hot-Dip Galvanizing

Heavy sacrificial zinc system

Popular for outdoor structures, supports and non-subsea exposure.

  • Thick coating affects thread fit
  • Oversized tapped nuts may be required
  • Not interchangeable with electro-zinc

Mechanical Zinc

Reduced hydrogen charging risk

Mechanical deposition can achieve comparatively thick zinc without electroplating.

  • Useful for high-strength steel
  • Rougher appearance than electro-zinc
  • Check thread fit after coating

Electro Zinc

Economical indoor / mild exposure

Suitable for moderate environments with suitable passivate / sealer systems.

  • Thin and dimensionally efficient
  • Not premium offshore protection by itself
  • Hydrogen embrittlement controls matter

Electroless Nickel

Uniform chemical deposit

Useful where chemical resistance and thickness uniformity are important.

  • Uniform over complex geometry
  • Project-specific thickness
  • Higher cost than basic zinc

Plain / Black / Phosphate

Process / temporary protection

Useful for assembly, storage and as undercoats; not a substitute for premium offshore systems.

  • Oil / wax preservation common
  • Phosphate can improve lubricant retention
  • Define storage period and environment
Do not publish a single “salt spray hours” promise for a coating family. Corrosion results depend on substrate, coating thickness, basecoat/topcoat, edge condition, cure, passivate/sealer, test criterion and project specification.

Regional Market & Procurement

Middle East, North America and India buy differently.

ASTM/ASME material language is widely shared, but approved-vendor systems, local content, documentation, stock strategy and coating expectations vary by region and project.

Middle East — buyer expectations

  • Strong ASTM A193/A194/A320 usage across refinery, petrochemical, LNG and upstream packages.
  • ISO 15156 / NACE and controlled-hardness material requirements frequently appear in sour-service projects.
  • PTFE/Xylan-type systems, HDG, zinc flake, zinc-nickel and CRA materials are widely specified depending on service.
  • Operator / EPC approved-vendor status and project-specific ITP approval are often commercially decisive.
  • Export seaworthy packing, heat-lot segregation and document completeness are expected.

Middle East — target markets

Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Oman, Kuwait and Bahrain combine refinery, petrochemical, LNG, offshore and pipeline demand.

Saudi Arabia UAE Qatar Oman Kuwait Bahrain
Commercial differentiator: fast quotation against operator/EPC specs, complete MTC package, coating traceability, PMI/NDT capability and fast project documentation.

North America — buyer expectations

  • ASTM/ASME terminology dominates pressure bolting, refinery and pipeline procurement.
  • Inch sizes, UNC/UNF and especially 8UN remain critical for large flange stud bolting.
  • MTR traceability and heat-lot documentation are standard expectations for critical packages.
  • Fast turnaround is important for maintenance, shutdown and turnaround supply.
  • Specialty distributors often stock B7, B8M, L7 and coated studs for MRO demand.

North America — commercial focus

Speed, exact ASME flange stud lengths, reliable coating friction and documentation accuracy can matter as much as unit price.

USA Canada Gulf Coast Refinery MRO Pipeline
Export opportunity: stock-standard ASTM materials with rapid made-to-length conversion and complete traceability can compete well when lead time is shorter than domestic special manufacture.

India — buyer expectations

  • Strong domestic manufacturing base for B7/B7M/B16, L7/L7M and A194 nut grades.
  • Indian refinery and EPC buyers often use ASTM/ASME/API/project specifications even when BIS standards are relevant elsewhere.
  • Third-party inspection, PMI, NACE-related hardness records and complete MTC documentation are major differentiators for critical packages.
  • India is a natural export base into GCC and Africa for alloy-steel and stainless stud bolting.
  • Duplex, super duplex and nickel-alloy work demands stronger material-source control and PMI discipline.

India — target sectors

Refinery, upstream, gas transmission, LNG, fertilizer, petrochemical, heavy process equipment and EPC package supply.

Refinery ONGC / Gas Petrochemical EPC GCC Export

Testing • QA • Traceability

Pressure bolting is a documentation product.

Critical oil & gas buyers need the fastener and the evidence behind it: raw material, heat treatment, mechanical properties, hardness, coating and lot traceability.

MTC / MTR

EN 10204 3.1; 3.2 / witnessed certification where the PO or project calls for it.

Mechanical Tests

Tensile, yield, elongation, reduction of area, hardness and nut proof-load as applicable.

Charpy Impact

Low-temperature grades and project-specific impact temperature verification.

PMI

Alloy verification for stainless, duplex and nickel-alloy components; frequency per ITP.

NDT

MPI, LPI or UT where required by grade, process, purchase specification or inspection plan.

Thread Gauging

GO/NO-GO, pitch, class/tolerance, lead, runout and post-coating fit.

Coating QA

Thickness, cure, adhesion, friction/torque-tension and corrosion testing per approved system.

Traceability

Raw-material heat → manufacture → heat treat → thread → coating → inspection → pack.

Marking • Packing • Export

Built for international project supply, not just factory dispatch.

Oil & gas fasteners are often stored outdoors, shipped by sea and received by EPC warehouses. Packing must protect threads, preserve coatings and maintain heat/lot segregation.

Typical export packing architecture

Lot SegregationSeparate heat numbers, grades and coating lots.
Thread ProtectionCaps, separators or protective wrapping where required.
Rust PreventionVCI / preservative oil / desiccant depending finish and transit.
Seaworthy PackingCrates / pallets suitable for export handling.
LabelingPO, item, grade, size, quantity, heat/lot and package number.
Document PackMTC, test reports, coating certificate, TPI release and packing list.

Marking requirements

Marking should be specification- and project-driven. Typical controls include grade symbol, manufacturer mark, heat/lot identity and project-specific color code only where approved.

Do not use color alone for traceability. Physical marking, tags and certification must maintain identity after coating and packing.
