01 · Process map

First choose
the right route.

“Fastener size” and “forging machine cutoff diameter” are not the same specification. Material, strength, upset ratio, head geometry, die stations, transfer, billet length, forming load and tooling determine the real boundary.

Cold Forming

Preferred for high-volume fasteners when the material can be formed at or near room temperature.

  • Excellent material utilisation
  • High production rates
  • Good surface finish and dimensional repeatability
  • Common route for screws, bolts and many nuts

Hot Forging

Used when section size, material strength, deformation severity or geometry makes cold forming inefficient or impractical.

  • Large bolts, heavy nuts and special forgings
  • Lower forming loads for heavy deformation
  • Suited to large bar/billet feed
  • Usually requires scale, heat and post-forge control

Adjacent Processes

Some products in a fastener catalogue are normally manufactured by other processes and should not be mislabeled as forged.

  • Washers: blanking / stamping / ring rolling
  • Studs: cut + chamfer + thread rolling
  • Threaded rod: bar/wire + continuous thread rolling
  • Special collars or ends may be upset/forged

05 · Engineering decision

Diameter alone
is not enough.

Before publishing a PMEW capability, validate the part against the actual machine and tooling envelope rather than copying the broad industry range.

01

Material

Carbon steel, alloy steel, stainless, titanium and nickel alloys can shift the feasible cold/hot boundary significantly.

02

Geometry

Head-to-shank volume, flange size, recess depth, extrusion and reductions can be more limiting than nominal thread diameter.

03

Machine

Cutoff diameter, cutoff length, forging load, station count, die space, transfer and ejector stroke must all fit.

04

Economics

Annual quantity, tooling cost, cycle rate, heat treatment, machining allowance and material yield determine the best route.

Important: This page is an engineering reference and UI framework, not a standards compliance certificate, quotation, or declaration of PMEW installed capacity. Nominal metric and inch ranges are intentionally presented as practical catalogue bands. Before converting them into a commercial “we manufacture” claim, replace the range limits with validated PMEW machine-by-machine data: material grade, maximum cutoff diameter/length, tonnage, station count, die diameter, KO stroke, induction/heating range, thread rolling capacity and quality approvals.
