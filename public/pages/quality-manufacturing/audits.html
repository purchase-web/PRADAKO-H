SEC 01 Quality at Every Level

Quality is verified through the complete manufacturing system.

Our Internal Quality Audit programme provides objective evidence that every relevant process — from incoming material and production to inspection, testing, traceability, packaging, and final release — is operating in accordance with defined procedures, customer requirements, applicable standards, and PMEW's quality expectations.

Internal Quality Audits help us look beyond the final product. They evaluate the complete system behind every fastener and engineered component, including the people, machines, methods, materials, measurements, records, controls, and decisions that influence product quality.

The objective is not only to identify nonconformities, but to prevent recurrence, reduce process variation, strengthen accountability, and improve overall manufacturing performance.

Every audited process must be

  • Properly defined and documented
  • Consistently implemented
  • Effectively controlled
  • Supported by objective records
  • Capable of meeting customer and regulatory requirements
  • Continuously improved through corrective and preventive action

SEC 04 Focused Audit Priority

A Risk-Based Audit Approach

PMEW uses a risk-based approach to determine audit frequency, depth, and priority. Processes with a greater influence on safety, product conformity, customer satisfaction, or regulatory compliance receive increased audit attention.

Not every process carries the same level of risk. Audit resources are focused where they can create the greatest improvement and risk-reduction impact.

Audit deeper where the impact is greater. Risk-based planning ensures that audit frequency and technical depth reflect the true importance of each process and its influence on customer requirements.

SEC 05 Structured Evaluation

Our Internal Quality Audit Cycle

From planning and evidence collection to corrective action and effectiveness verification, every audit follows a disciplined and traceable process.

01

Audit Planning

The audit programme is prepared based on process risk, organisational priorities, applicable requirements, previous findings, and customer expectations. Each audit defines its scope, objectives, criteria, timing, and responsible auditor.

02

Audit Preparation

The auditor reviews procedures, process documents, previous reports, performance data, complaints, nonconformities, corrective actions, and applicable customer requirements.

03

On-Site Evaluation

Objective evidence is gathered through observation, interviews, record verification, document review, product sampling, traceability checks, equipment verification, and process parameter review.

04

Findings and Reporting

Findings are documented clearly and communicated to process owners. They may include conformity, opportunities for improvement, observations, minor nonconformities, major nonconformities, or systemic concerns.

05

Corrective Action

Responsible teams contain immediate risk, determine the root cause, and implement appropriate corrective action. Actions are expected to address the underlying weakness — not only the visible symptom.

06

Verification and Closure

Findings are closed only after evidence confirms that the required action has been completed and is effective — through follow-up audits, record review, product verification, performance analysis, or recurrence monitoring.

Auditor Independence and Competence

Internal auditors are selected based on knowledge, training, process understanding, objectivity, and audit competence. Where practical, auditors do not audit their own work.

  • Quality management systems
  • Manufacturing processes and product standards
  • Customer-specific requirements
  • Core quality tools and risk-based thinking
  • Root cause analysis and corrective action methods
  • Professional audit techniques

From Findings to Measurable Improvement

The value of an audit is determined by what happens after the report is issued. Findings become direct inputs for stronger processes and improved manufacturing performance.

  • Corrective and preventive action
  • Process improvement and employee training
  • Procedure and control plan revision
  • Error-proofing and automation opportunities
  • Supplier development and maintenance planning
  • Management review and resource allocation
