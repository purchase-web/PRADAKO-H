Technical Overview

Material grade is a performance decision.

The correct fastener grade ensures predictable tightening, safe preload generation, fatigue resistance, corrosion suitability and long-term joint reliability. A fastener should not be selected only by size or shape; its material class must match the load, environment, coating process and assembly method.

01 / Strength

Mechanical load capacity

Property classes define tensile strength, yield strength, proof load and hardness. These values help engineers choose fasteners that can safely maintain clamping force.

02 / Process

Manufacturing compatibility

Heat treatment, electroplating, coating, lubrication and tightening methods must be suitable for the selected grade, especially in high-strength fasteners.

03 / Environment

Corrosion performance

Stainless grades and engineered coatings protect fasteners from moisture, chemicals, coastal exposure, oxidation and long-term environmental degradation.

Property Class 8.8

Reliable high-tensile strength for general engineering.

Property Class 8.8 is one of the most widely used high-tensile fastener classes. It provides a strong balance of strength, toughness, ductility and cost efficiency, making it suitable for broad industrial use.

Nominal tensile 800 MPa
Yield ratio 0.8
Material type Carbon / alloy steel
Condition Quenched & tempered

Key characteristics

Class 8.8 fasteners deliver dependable clamping force while maintaining useful ductility before failure. They are easier to manage during coating, tightening and field service compared with very high-strength classes.

Suitable applications

  • Automotive assemblies
  • General machinery
  • Industrial frames and brackets
  • Agricultural equipment
  • Fabricated assemblies
  • Construction machinery

Selection guidance

Use Property Class 8.8 when the application requires reliable high-tensile performance, repeatable tightening behaviour, good ductility and broad coating compatibility.

Property Class 10.9

Higher strength for demanding mechanical assemblies.

Property Class 10.9 fasteners are designed for applications requiring higher tensile strength and higher clamp load than Class 8.8. They are used in performance-critical bolted joints where stronger preload is required.

Nominal tensile 1000 MPa
Yield ratio 0.9
Material type Alloy steel
Condition Quenched & tempered

Key characteristics

Class 10.9 provides higher clamp force and greater load-carrying capability than Class 8.8. It is ideal where joint design requires compact fasteners with higher mechanical strength.

Suitable applications

  • Heavy-duty automotive systems
  • Engine and transmission assemblies
  • Suspension and chassis systems
  • Mining and earthmoving equipment
  • Railway equipment
  • High-preload bolted joints

Important consideration

Because Class 10.9 is a higher strength grade, coating and plating processes must be carefully controlled. Hydrogen embrittlement risk becomes more important, especially where electroplating or acid cleaning is involved.

Selection guidance

Use Property Class 10.9 when the joint requires higher preload, improved load resistance and compact high-strength fastening where Class 8.8 may not be sufficient.

Property Class 12.9

Maximum strength for compact high-load fastening.

Property Class 12.9 is among the highest commonly used metric property classes for carbon and alloy steel fasteners. It is engineered for very high tensile capacity, high clamp load and demanding mechanical performance.

Nominal tensile 1200 MPa
Strength level Ultra high tensile
Material type High-strength alloy steel
Condition Quenched & tempered

Key characteristics

Class 12.9 fasteners provide very high tensile capacity and excellent clamp load potential. They are useful where space is limited and the fastener must carry high load without increasing diameter.

Suitable applications

  • Socket head cap screws
  • Tooling and die fixtures
  • Mould assemblies
  • Precision machinery
  • Hydraulic systems
  • High-pressure mechanical systems

Important consideration

Class 12.9 requires careful handling in design, coating, tightening and service. It is more sensitive to hydrogen embrittlement, over-tightening, surface damage and improper coating selection.

Selection guidance

Use Property Class 12.9 only when the application truly demands maximum strength and when tightening, lubrication, mating material and coating compatibility are properly controlled.

Stainless Steel Grades

Corrosion-resistant fastening for demanding environments.

Stainless steel fasteners are selected primarily for corrosion resistance, hygiene, appearance, temperature behaviour and long service life. Unlike carbon steel fasteners, stainless steel fasteners are not identified as 8.8, 10.9 or 12.9.

A2 / A4 / Duplex

Grade first. Strength class second.

Stainless steel fasteners commonly use designations such as A2-70 or A4-80. The first part identifies the stainless grade and corrosion behaviour, while the number indicates the strength class.

Example: A2-70 means an A2 stainless steel fastener with approximately 700 MPa minimum tensile strength. A4-80 means an A4 stainless steel fastener with a higher strength class of approximately 800 MPa.
A1

Free-machining stainless

Used where machinability is important. Corrosion resistance is generally lower than A2 and A4, making it suitable for selected machined fastener parts.

A2

General-purpose stainless steel

A2 stainless is widely used for indoor, outdoor, machinery, food equipment, enclosures and general corrosion-resistant industrial fastening.

A4

Enhanced corrosion resistance

A4 stainless is preferred for marine, coastal, chemical, wastewater and aggressive outdoor applications due to improved chloride resistance.

F / C

Ferritic and martensitic stainless

These grades are selected for specific mechanical, magnetic or hardening requirements. Corrosion resistance and strength vary by grade and heat treatment.

Duplex

Austenitic-ferritic stainless

Duplex stainless provides higher strength and improved resistance to stress corrosion cracking in offshore, energy, chemical and structural environments.

Selection Guide

Choose by application requirement.

A higher property class is not automatically the best choice. The correct selection depends on load requirement, installation method, corrosion exposure, coating process, mating material and whether the joint will be serviced or reassembled.

Requirement Recommended Material Class Reason
General high-tensile fastening Property Class 8.8 Balanced strength, ductility and broad industrial usability.
Higher clamp load and critical machinery Property Class 10.9 Improved load resistance for compact or heavy-duty joints.
Maximum strength in compact assemblies Property Class 12.9 Very high tensile strength for tooling, moulds and socket screw applications.
General corrosion resistance A2 stainless steel Suitable for normal atmospheric, indoor, outdoor and mildly wet environments.
Marine, chemical or coastal resistance A4 stainless steel Improved resistance in chloride, salt, moisture and chemical exposure.
High corrosion plus high strength A4-80, duplex stainless or engineered coating systems Useful where both mechanical strength and corrosion resistance are critical.
