Engineering Perspective

Long-term corrosion protection,
engineered as a system.

Hot Dip Galvanising is approached as a long-term corrosion protection system, where steel fasteners are coated through immersion in molten zinc, forming a metallurgically bonded multi-layer structure. This process is not viewed as a surface finish alone, but as a system-driven engineering solution, where coating thickness, environmental exposure, material grade, and service life are aligned to ensure consistent field performance.

The objective is to deliver robust, maintenance-efficient protection across demanding applications including infrastructure, energy systems, and outdoor structural assemblies.

Process Overview

A controlled route from
surface preparation to stabilisation.

02 / 10
01

Surface Preparation

Surface preparation is treated as the foundation of coating performance, ensuring complete removal of contaminants and oxides prior to galvanising. The process typically includes degreasing and cleaning to eliminate oils and organic residues, pickling to remove mill scale and oxides, and fluxing to activate the surface and prevent re-oxidation.

This stage ensures that the steel surface is chemically clean and reactive, enabling proper metallurgical bonding during immersion.
02

Molten Zinc Immersion

Fasteners are immersed in molten zinc maintained within controlled temperature ranges, enabling a diffusion-based reaction between zinc and iron. During this stage, the coating develops through intermetallic layer formation, creating a structure that is fundamentally different from conventional coatings.

03

Metallurgical Layer Formation

The galvanised coating consists of a graded multi-layer system, each layer contributing to adhesion and durability:

01 Gamma (Γ) Initial zinc-iron interface providing strong adhesion
02 Delta (δ) Dense alloy layer contributing to coating integrity
03 Zeta (ζ) Intermediate layer adding thickness and durability
04 Eta (η) Outer pure zinc layer offering sacrificial protection

This layered structure ensures that the coating is integrally bonded to the base metal, rather than mechanically attached.

04

Cooling and Stabilisation

After withdrawal, the coating solidifies and stabilises, forming a uniform, continuous protective layer across the entire component, including edges, threads, and geometrical complexities.

Corrosion Protection Mechanism

Barrier, sacrificial action,
and stable patina formation.

04 / 10

Barrier Protection

The zinc coating isolates the steel from environmental exposure.

Sacrificial Protection

Zinc preferentially corrodes, protecting the underlying steel even if the coating is locally damaged.

Patina Formation

Exposure to atmosphere leads to formation of zinc carbonate, creating a stable and durable protective layer.

This combination ensures long-term corrosion resistance with minimal maintenance requirements.

Material and Application Considerations

Hot dip galvanising is selected based on material grade, mechanical requirements, and service environment:

  • Suitable for carbon steel fasteners across structural applications
  • Application-dependent evaluation for higher strength grades
  • Controlled processing for components exposed to cyclic loading or critical stress conditions

The selection process ensures that the coating system aligns with functional, mechanical, and environmental requirements.
