Overview

Why Material Selection Matters

A fastener is only reliable when its material is correctly matched with load, environment, assembly method, coating requirement, and service conditions.

Engineering Reminder The same product type can perform very differently depending on the material. A bolt used in a dry indoor assembly may require an entirely different material or coating for marine, chemical, structural, or high-temperature environments.
  • Mechanical Load Material determines tensile strength, yield strength, clamp load, fatigue resistance, and hardness.
  • Corrosion Exposure Outdoor, coastal, chemical, and humid environments need corrosion-conscious material selection.
  • Manufacturing Process Cold forming, thread rolling, heat treatment, machining, and plating must suit the material grade.
  • Assembly Behavior Torque, friction, galling risk, coating thickness, and joint design influence final performance.

01 Low Carbon Steel

Mild Steel

Practical, economical, highly formable, and suitable for general-purpose fastener production.

Best Used When
  • Economical fastening is required.
  • Application needs good formability.
  • Coating will provide corrosion protection.
  • Extreme tensile strength is not the main requirement.

Overview

Mild Steel, also known as low-carbon steel, is one of the most widely used materials for general-purpose fasteners. It offers a strong balance of cost efficiency, ductility, machinability, cold-forming ability, and coating compatibility.

Because of its relatively low carbon content, mild steel is easier to form, forge, thread, bend, and process compared to higher-strength steels.

Material Type Low-carbon / plain carbon steel
Strength Level Low to medium strength
Formability Excellent for cold forming and thread rolling
Corrosion Resistance Low in uncoated condition

Common Applications

General engineering Sheet metal fastening Furniture assemblies Electrical panels Fabrication work Light construction

Typical Fastener Products

Screws Machine screws, self-tapping screws, sheet metal screws, wood screws.
Bolts, Nuts & Rods Hex bolts, carriage bolts, nuts, studs, and threaded rods.
Washers & General Components Plain washers, brackets, clamps, frames, housings, and economical custom parts.

Suitable Surface Finishes

Finish Purpose
Zinc Plating General corrosion protection and clean appearance.
Hot Dip Galvanizing Heavy-duty outdoor corrosion protection.
Phosphating Improves lubricity and supports paint adhesion.
Black Oxide Decorative black finish with light protection.
Zinc Nickel / Special Coatings Used when higher corrosion resistance or application-specific performance is needed.
Not Recommended When Mild steel is not ideal for highly corrosive, marine, chemical, high-temperature, or high-tensile applications unless the correct coating or alternate material is selected.
02 High Strength Steel

Alloy Steel

High-strength material for demanding fastening applications requiring load capacity, fatigue resistance, hardness, and durability.

Best Used When
  • High tensile strength is required.
  • Joint demands high clamp load.
  • Application involves machinery or structural loads.
  • Heat treatment and hardness control are required.

Overview

Alloy Steel is used where fasteners require higher tensile strength, fatigue resistance, hardness, toughness, and load-bearing capability. Alloying elements such as chromium, molybdenum, nickel, manganese, or vanadium may be used depending on performance requirements.

In fasteners, alloy steel is commonly used for higher property classes such as 8.8, 10.9, and 12.9, as well as high-strength industrial and structural grades.

Material Type Steel with controlled alloying elements
Strength Level Medium to very high strength
Heat Treatment Often quenched and tempered
Coating Need Usually required for corrosion protection

Common Property Classes & Grades

Class / Grade Typical Use
Property Class 8.8 High-strength general engineering fasteners.
Property Class 10.9 Automotive, machinery, and high-load assemblies.
Property Class 12.9 Very high-strength socket screws and precision assemblies.
ASTM A193 B7 High-temperature and pressure service bolting.
ASTM A320 L7 Low-temperature service bolting.

Common Applications

Automotive assemblies Heavy machinery Railways Structural connections Oil & gas assemblies Renewable energy systems

Typical Fastener Products

High-Tensile Bolts Hex bolts, structural bolts, flange bolts, and high-load mechanical fasteners.
Socket & Precision Fasteners Socket head cap screws, set screws, shoulder screws, and precision assemblies.
Studs, Rods & Nuts Stud bolts, threaded rods, high-strength nuts, and washers for structural assemblies.

Surface Protection

Finish Purpose
Zinc Plating General corrosion protection for suitable strength levels.
Zinc Nickel High corrosion resistance for automotive and demanding environments.
Zinc Flake High-performance coating commonly used for high-strength fasteners.
Phosphating Lubrication support and controlled friction behavior.
Hot Dip Galvanizing Used for structural and outdoor applications where suitable.
Engineering Note High-strength alloy steel fasteners require controlled heat treatment, hardness verification, thread accuracy, and surface treatment control. Hydrogen embrittlement risk must be managed during electroplating of high-strength parts.
03 Corrosion Resistant

Stainless Steel

Corrosion-resistant material for long-life fastening systems used in outdoor, marine, hygienic, and industrial environments.

Best Used When
  • Corrosion resistance is a priority.
  • Application is outdoor, coastal, or chemical.
  • Clean appearance is required.
  • Coating-free service life is preferred.

Overview

Stainless Steel is selected where corrosion resistance, hygiene, appearance, durability, and long service life are important. Chromium in stainless steel forms a passive protective layer on the surface, helping resist rust and corrosion.

Stainless steel fasteners are widely used in outdoor, marine, food processing, chemical, pharmaceutical, architectural, and clean-environment applications.

Material Type Corrosion-resistant steel alloy
Corrosion Resistance Good to excellent depending on grade
Coating Need Usually used without coating
Appearance Clean, bright, and premium

Common Stainless Steel Grades

Grade Description Typical Use
A2 / 304 General-purpose stainless steel. Indoor, outdoor, machinery, architecture.
A4 / 316 Higher corrosion resistance due to molybdenum content. Marine, coastal, chemical, industrial environments.
A2-70 A2 stainless with 700 MPa nominal tensile class. General stainless fasteners.
A4-70 / A4-80 Higher corrosion-resistant stainless property classes. Demanding corrosion-resistant assemblies.
410 / Martensitic SS Hardenable stainless steel. Self-drilling screws and cutting applications.

Common Applications

Marine environments Food processing Pharmaceutical equipment Water treatment Solar structures Architectural facades

Typical Fastener Products

Stainless Screws Self-tapping screws, self-drilling screws, machine screws, and socket screws.
Bolts, Studs & Rods Hex bolts, threaded rods, studs, and custom corrosion-resistant fasteners.
Nuts, Washers & Rivets Stainless nuts, washers, rivets, and assembled fastening systems.
Engineering Note Stainless steel fasteners can be prone to galling, especially in stainless-to-stainless threaded assemblies. Correct thread quality, lubrication, surface finish, and assembly speed help reduce galling risk.
04 Non-Ferrous Alloy

Brass

Copper-zinc alloy used where machinability, conductivity, corrosion resistance, non-magnetic behavior, and decorative appearance matter.

Best Used When
  • Electrical conductivity is required.
  • Decorative appearance matters.
  • Precision machining is important.
  • Non-magnetic behavior is preferred.

Overview

Brass is a copper-zinc alloy known for corrosion resistance, machinability, electrical conductivity, thermal conductivity, and attractive appearance. It is widely used where fasteners must perform in electrical, decorative, plumbing, instrumentation, and precision applications.

Material Type Copper-zinc alloy
Machinability Excellent for turned components
Magnetism Non-magnetic
Strength Level Lower than alloy steel

Common Brass Types

Grade / Type Typical Use
Free-Cutting Brass Machined screws, inserts, standoffs, and turned components.
Cartridge Brass Formed parts and light-duty fasteners.
Naval Brass Selected marine-related and corrosion-resistant uses.
Lead-Free Brass Plumbing, water-contact, and regulated applications.
Nickel-Plated Brass Electrical, decorative, and appearance-focused applications.

Common Applications

Electrical terminals Switchgear Instrumentation Decorative hardware Plumbing fittings Precision components

Typical Fastener Products

Electrical Fasteners Electrical screws, brass nuts, washers, terminals, and switchgear components.
Turned Parts Threaded inserts, spacers, standoffs, bushes, and precision machined components.
Decorative Components Visible fasteners and fittings where appearance and corrosion resistance are important.
Not Recommended When Brass is not normally selected for high-tensile, heavy structural, or high-vibration fastening where very high mechanical strength is required.
