Protection is functional.
Not decorative.

A properly selected coating must resist corrosion while preserving thread fit, dimensional conformity, recess function, friction, clamp load, adhesion and assembly compatibility.

A coating that survives salt spray but produces thread interference, unstable tightening or delayed hydrogen-assisted failure is not an acceptable fastener coating.

02 · Interpretation

What the test reveals—and what it cannot promise.

Salt spray is a controlled comparative test. It is powerful for detecting coating defects and process inconsistency, but it cannot independently predict service life.

It evaluates

Comparative coating performance

  • Pores, discontinuities and uncoated areas
  • Passivation, sealing and topcoat consistency
  • Thin areas and coating-distribution problems
  • Batch-to-batch process stability
  • Handling or assembly damage
  • Compliance with a defined drawing or specification
  • White corrosion and basis-metal corrosion as separate endpoints
It does not establish

Guaranteed years in service

  • A universal hours-to-years conversion
  • Performance under every wet/dry cycle
  • Resistance to UV, chemicals or mechanical wear unless separately assessed
  • Galvanic compatibility with the assembled structure
  • Torque–tension performance after installation
  • Environmental hydrogen embrittlement resistance
  • Equivalence between NSS and cyclic corrosion rankings
03 · Test environments

Choose the mechanism—not merely the harsher acronym.

NSS, AASS, CASS, cyclic corrosion and sulfur-dioxide condensation tests are not interchangeable. The drawing or product specification must select the relevant method.

NSS

Neutral Salt Spray

Continuous chloride fog · comparative corrosion resistance

Best suited to

Zinc, zinc-alloy, zinc-flake and many plated or coated steel fasteners when NSS is specified by the coating or customer standard.

Core references

ASTM B117-26ISO 9227:2022 + Amd 1:2024IS 5528:2024

Engineering caution

The chamber method does not prescribe exposure duration or acceptance. Testing must state the specimen condition, significant surfaces and separate white/red corrosion criteria.

Do not assume

NSS performance is not automatically equivalent to cyclic-corrosion, outdoor or road-salt performance.

AASS

Acetic-Acid Salt Spray

Acidified salt atmosphere

Use

Selected metallic and decorative coating systems where an acidified test is explicitly required.

Reference

ISO 9227

Control

Do not treat AASS as the default for ordinary zinc-coated steel fasteners. Product-specific requirements govern its suitability and acceptance.

CASS

Copper-Accelerated AASS

Aggressive decorative-coating test

Use

Selected nickel/chromium, anodized and decorative systems when the governing specification expressly requires CASS.

Reference

ISO 9227

Control

CASS severity does not make it a universal substitute for NSS. Different mechanisms may produce different coating rankings.

CCT

Cyclic Corrosion Testing

Salt · humidity · drying · temperature stages

Best suited to

Automotive, commercial vehicle, de-icing salt and selected exterior applications where wet/dry cycling better represents field mechanisms.

References

ISO 16701:2025ISO 14993:2026SAE J2334_201604

Engineering caution

A high NSS result does not guarantee the same ranking in cyclic testing. The coating, substrate and design must be validated using the specified cycle.

SO₂

Kesternich-Type Test

Humid sulfur-dioxide atmosphere · industrial pollution

Current reference

ISO 22479:2019 is the current ISO fixed-gas sulfur-dioxide test. ISO 6988 is withdrawn. DIN 50018:2013 is withdrawn and DIN recommends DIN EN ISO 22479.

ISO 22479:2019ASTM G87DIN EN ISO 22479

Fastener use

ISO 4042 and ISO 10683 treat the sulfur-dioxide test as relevant to outdoor building fasteners when required. The number of cycles must be agreed or defined by the governing product specification.

04 · Fastener geometry

A fastener is not a flat laboratory panel.

Threads, recesses, edges and interfaces create coating-distribution and damage mechanisms that a flat panel cannot reproduce.

01

Thread roots and crests

Deposit distribution, clearance and gaugeability must remain controlled after coating.

02

Recesses and blind holes

Pooling, incomplete coverage and trapped chemistry can affect corrosion and tool engagement.

03

Bearing and washer interfaces

Assembly can abrade the finish and create shielded or crevice conditions.

04

Bulk-processing contact marks

Barrel handling, feeding, sorting, packaging and transport can reduce as-coated performance.

05

Installation damage

Wrenching surfaces and recesses should be evaluated in the condition required by the application.

08 · Hot-dip galvanizing

Thick zinc protection needs thread engineering.

Hot-dip galvanizing is not simply “more zinc plating”. It produces zinc–iron alloy layers and materially changes thread allowance, friction and assembly behaviour.

HDG

Barrier protection + sacrificial protection

ISO 10684 applies principally to spun hot-dip galvanized coarse-thread steel fasteners from M8 to M64 and does not recommend galvanizing threaded fasteners below M8 or below 1.25 mm pitch. ASTM F2329/F2329M is the fastener-specific ASTM route.

Thread allowance

Coating thickness can interfere with ordinary threads. Use the specified oversize internal thread or other approved allowance and gauge the coated assembly.

Assembly system

Supply compatible galvanized bolts, nuts and washers. Uncontrolled substitution can alter fit, coating damage, friction and reliability.

Torque / preload

Do not reuse torque values validated for bare fasteners. The exact galvanized and lubricated assembly requires an appropriate installation method.

Primary conformity

Control coating thickness or mass, coverage, finish, appearance, adhesion, thread fit and embrittlement-related requirements.

White storage stain

Dry before packing, prevent trapped moisture and provide ventilation. Light zinc staining is not the same as basis-metal corrosion.

Duplex protection

A compatible organic coating over galvanizing can add a barrier, but adhesion, repair, edge coverage and threaded-surface friction must be qualified.

09 · Safety-critical control

Hydrogen embrittlement cannot be inspected away.

High-strength steel fasteners can absorb hydrogen during cleaning, pickling, electroplating or service corrosion and may fail later under sustained tensile stress.

01

Susceptible material

Strength, hardness, microstructure and residual stress determine susceptibility.

02

Hydrogen entry

Acid cleaning, pickling, cathodic cleaning, electroplating or corrosion can introduce hydrogen.

03

Delay / diffusion

A fastener may pass ordinary inspection while diffusible hydrogen remains present.

04

Sustained stress

Preload, residual stress and service loading can trigger delayed cracking.

05

Delayed failure

Failure can occur suddenly with little visible plastic deformation.

Baking reduces risk.
It is not an absolute guarantee.

Control the complete process: pretreatment, hardness limits, delay before baking, baking temperature/time, verification testing and the distinction between internal hydrogen embrittlement and environmental hydrogen embrittlement. Key references include ISO 4042, ASTM F1941/F1941M, ASTM F1940, ISO 15330 and ISO/TR 20491.

“Passed 500 hours” is not a complete certificate.

  6. Coating batch

Specify the system.
Not only the hours.

Technically reliable corrosion protection coordinates material, strength level, pretreatment, coating composition, thickness, conversion treatment, sealer, topcoat, lubricant, curing, dimensional allowance, hydrogen control, testing, packaging and assembly.

The best requirement is not the one claiming the largest number. It is the one that defines the correct mechanism, preserves fastener function and establishes measurable acceptance criteria for the intended application.
