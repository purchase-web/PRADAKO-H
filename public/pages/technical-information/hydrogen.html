Fastener Reliability Protection

Hydrogen risk is invisible.
Relief must be controlled.

Hydrogen embrittlement is a delayed cracking risk that can affect high-strength steel fasteners when atomic hydrogen enters the material during surface preparation, acid cleaning, pickling, electroplating or certain coating processes. Relief baking is used to drive out diffusible hydrogen before the fastener enters service.

Hydrogen Entry Points

Where the risk begins.

01 / 10
Surface preparation

Acid pickling

Acid cleaning and pickling can introduce atomic hydrogen into steel surfaces if not controlled properly.

Coating route

Electroplating

Zinc and other electrolytic coating processes can generate hydrogen at the fastener surface.

Material condition

High hardness

Higher-strength and higher-hardness steels are generally more susceptible to hydrogen-related delayed fracture.

Assembly condition

Tensile stress

Preload, tightening stress and service stress can support crack initiation in susceptible parts.

Process delay

Late baking

Delayed transfer from plating to baking can reduce the effectiveness of hydrogen relief.

Documentation

Weak traceability

Missing bake logs, oven records or batch linkage reduces confidence in critical fastener release.

The Pradako Approach
02 / 10

Prevention first.
Relief second.

At Pradako Mechanical & Engineering Works, hydrogen embrittlement control is treated as a process discipline, not as a finishing afterthought. The aim is to reduce hydrogen entry wherever possible and then apply controlled thermal relief wherever the fastener, coating or customer specification requires it.

This is especially important for property class 10.9, 12.9 and other high-strength fasteners used in automotive, railway, construction, industrial and safety-critical assemblies.

Hydrogen embrittlement control depends on three factors: susceptible material, hydrogen source and tensile stress. Remove or control one of these factors and the risk is reduced.
Susceptible material High strength, high hardness or hardened steel.
Hydrogen source Pickling, plating, cleaning or corrosive exposure.
Tensile stress Preload, clamp load or service loading.
Relief baking: controlled heat treatment to reduce diffusible hydrogen before service loading.
Application Scope
03 / 10

For fasteners where
delayed failure is not acceptable.

Hydrogen embrittlement relief is commonly specified for high-strength fasteners after electroplating or other hydrogen-generating operations. The process is particularly relevant for bolts, screws, studs, washers and threaded components used under clamp load or tensile stress.

The relief cycle is selected according to material grade, hardness, coating type, part geometry, customer requirement and applicable standard. For critical applications, process records and testing references are maintained as part of the release package.

High-tensile bolts Property class 10.9, 12.9 and similar grades.
Threaded assemblies Parts exposed to preload, clamp force or service stress.
Electroplated fasteners Zinc, zinc alloy and other electrolytic finishes.
Critical documentation Batch-wise oven records, release notes and inspection logs.
Process Overview

Controlled thermal relief.

04 / 10

Hydrogen embrittlement relief is generally performed by baking parts after plating or coating. The objective is to allow absorbed hydrogen to diffuse out of the material before it can contribute to delayed cracking under stress.

  • ✓Fast transfer from plating or coating operation to baking oven
  • ✓Defined oven temperature and soak duration as per specification
  • ✓Load arrangement controlled for proper heat exposure
  • ✓Oven temperature records maintained batch-wise
  • ✓Relief completion recorded before final release or dispatch

Critical process controls

Time to bake Transfer quickly after plating or coating operation.
Temperature window Controlled oven temperature as per standard or customer specification.
Load discipline Uniform load depth and part separation for consistent relief.

The exact cycle should always follow the relevant coating standard, customer drawing, OEM specification or approved control plan.
