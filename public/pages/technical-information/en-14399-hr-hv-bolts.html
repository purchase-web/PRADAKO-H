1. Executive Summary
Under EN 14399 (European standard for high-strength structural bolting), two primary assembly types dominate preloaded bolted joints: HR (Round head with cup square neck) and HV (Heavy hexagon head). While both deliver preload and ductility, their geometry, tightening methods, and inspection protocols differ critically. Selecting the wrong assembly leads to joint relaxation, hidden gaps, or non-compliant slip factors.
Key Principle: EN 14399 mandates that HR and HV assemblies are system components—the bolt, nut, and washer(s) are tested as a set. Mixing components from different manufacturers or standards voids compliance.

3. Geometry & Anti-Rotation Mechanism (Critical Difference)

HR Bolt (EN 14399-3)

  • Cup square neck directly under the round head.
  • Square neck engages fitted hole or punched square recess.
  • Result: No need to hold bolt head during tightening — ideal for blind assembly or single-sided access.

HV Bolt (EN 14399-4)

  • Standard heavy hex head (larger bearing area).
  • Unique width across flats: M12=22mm, M20=32mm (deviates from ISO 272).
  • Large under-head radius → improves fatigue strength.
  • Result: Requires torque wrench or backup wrench on bolt head (unless rotation-controlled).
⚠️ Common mistake: Using an HV bolt in a clearance hole without a backup wrench causes the bolt to spin, preventing preload.
4. Mechanical & Preload Requirements (EN 14399-2)
Both assemblies must meet preload values defined in EN 14399-2. Preload is a force (Fp,C) verified by: torque method, combined method (torque+angle), DTI/S10T, or hydraulic tensioning.

The nut decarburization limit is unique to EN 14399 — not found in general-purpose nut standards — and ensures ductility under preload.

6. Washer Configuration — Most Common Error

HR

  • One under nut + one under head (2 washers total)
  • Exception: friction surfaces tested & approved for direct bearing (rare)

HV

  • One under the rotated element (usually nut)
  • If bolt head rotated → place washer under head instead
❌ Incorrect: Placing only one washer under the nut for an HR bolt. That violates EN 14399-3 clause 6.2.
Washer Standards: EN 14399-5 (hardened flat washers) / EN 14399-6 (chamfered washers) — Marking: H on all EN 14399 washers.

9. Single Manufacturer Requirement — Mandatory
"Preloaded bolted assemblies are very sensitive to differences in manufacture and lubrication. Therefore it is important that the assembly is supplied by one manufacturer who is always responsible for the function of the assembly."
Consequence: Mixing bolts from Manufacturer A with nuts from Manufacturer B — even if both meet EN 14399 individually — is non-compliant unless tested as a complete system per EN 14399-2.
10. Inspection & Acceptance Criteria (EN 1090-2 + EN 14399)
Mandatory checks after tightening:
  • No gap visible between joint plies (0.2 mm feeler gauge rejection).
  • Nut rotation from snug-tight condition meets specified angle (e.g., 120° for M20 10.9).
  • Striking test (hammer test) — allowed only as quick check, not final QA.
  • Calibrated torque checking (only if torque-angle was used for installation).
Rejection conditions:
  • Mixed HR / HV components.
  • Missing washers.
  • Thread damage (galvanized spatter, cross-threading).
  • Bolt shank not extending beyond nut ≥ 1 thread.
  • Missing or incorrect marking (e.g., HV bolt without "HV").
  • Components from different manufacturers in same assembly.

12. Practical Example — Specification Clause (for tender)
"All preloaded bolted joints shall comply with EN 1090-2. Bolting assemblies shall be either HR (EN 14399-3) or HV (EN 14399-4) as shown on drawings. For connections marked 'HR assembly' — use cup square neck bolts with two washers. For HV assemblies — use one washer under the turned element. Do not mix HR and HV components. Do not mix components from different manufacturers. Provide batch certificates EN 10204 3.1 for each assembly part. All HV bolts of property class 10.9 shall be marked '10.9 HV' and nuts marked '10 HV'."
13. Final Technical Verdict
• Choose HR when: single-side access, slotted holes, or high seismic risk (anti-rotation backup).
• Choose HV when: open access, cost sensitivity, dynamic loads (fatigue benefit), or standard torque tools are available.
Both achieve the same preload and same slip factor if EN 14399 system components are used correctly. The difference is mechanical installation geometry — not strength.
